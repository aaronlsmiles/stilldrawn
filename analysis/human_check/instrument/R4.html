Rating task — about 25 minutes

Thank you for helping. There are two parts. Please do them in one sitting, and don't discuss them with anyone first.

  • Part 1 — 14 still drawings. One question about each. Roughly 3 minutes. This part is just the drawings on their own.
  • Part 2 — 20 short clips, each one made by animating one of those drawings. Two questions about each. Roughly 20 minutes.

Part 1 begins below. The bar at the top of the page tracks how far through you are.

“Does this original still image look like a hand-drawn black-and-white ink picture?”

5 = yes, completely · 1 = no, not at all

  1. No: it doesn't look hand-drawn — photographic, painted, or mechanically rendered
  2. Mostly not
  3. Half and half
  4. Mostly
  5. Yes: it could be a drawn panel

Ink can stop looking hand-drawn without becoming photographic. Look for marks that no one drawing by hand would make: contours gone soft or blurred, edges that fade instead of ending crisply, lines that smear, warp or run together in ways a pen or brush would not, or shading that has turned into a smooth gradient instead of line or tone. Clean, even linework, flat solid blacks and regular screentone are normal in manga and are not, by themselves, a departure.

Rating 20 short clips — about 20 minutes

Each screen shows an original still image (left) and a 5-second animated clip made from it (right, plays on loop). You answer two questions about each clip.

1. “Does this clip still look like a hand-drawn black-and-white ink picture, all the way through?”

5 = yes, completely · 1 = no, not at all

  1. No: it stops looking hand-drawn — photographic, painted, or mechanically rendered
  2. Mostly not
  3. Half and half
  4. Mostly: a few frames slip
  5. Yes: every frame could be a drawn panel

Ink can stop looking hand-drawn without becoming photographic. Look for marks that no one drawing by hand would make: contours gone soft or blurred, edges that fade instead of ending crisply, lines that smear, warp or run together in ways a pen or brush would not, or shading that has turned into a smooth gradient instead of line or tone. Clean, even linework, flat solid blacks and regular screentone are normal in manga and are not, by themselves, a departure.

A slight overall shift in paper tone or brightness belongs here, mildly — a 4 rather than a 5. It is not a question-2 change.

2. “Apart from the movement itself, is this the same original image throughout?”

5 = yes, it is unchanged · 1 = no, it became a different picture

  1. No: it became a different picture
  2. Mostly not: much of it is different
  3. Half and half
  4. Mostly: small things change
  5. Yes: nothing has been added, removed or replaced

Anything added, removed or replaced counts as a change, even if the setting looks identical — a clip that adds a person to an empty landscape is not the same image.

Action already in the drawing doesn't count. If the original shows glass shattering, a figure mid-leap, or cloth blowing, then that action carrying on is the animation working — not a change. Count only things the original never contained: a new person, vehicle, object or lettering.

Watch the whole five seconds before answering. Things that appear briefly and then leave still count.

The two are different questions and often have different answers. A clip can still look perfectly hand-drawn (a high answer to 1) while having turned into a completely different picture (a low answer to 2). How much a clip moves is not being asked about — a clip that barely moves but keeps everything intact is still a 5 on question 2.

Go with your eye — there are no right answers, and other people's answers will differ. Please do it in one sitting, without discussing it with anyone first. You can change an answer with Back before finishing.
